Additional Information & Instructions
The "Intent to File" (Locking in Your Backpay)

What is it? Think of this as "planting a flag." It tells the VA: "I am going to file a claim soon." * Why is it the most important step? The day you submit this is the day your backpay starts. If it takes you 364 days to actually finish your paperwork, the VA will owe you a check for that entire year once you're rated.

How to do it (Takes 5 Minutes):

Option A (Online): Log into VA.gov. Start a new disability claim. The moment you "start" the application, the VA automatically records an "Intent to File." You don't even have to finish the application that day.

Option B (By Phone): Call the VA at 1-800-827-1000 (M-F, 8 AM – 9 PM ET). Tell them: "I want to submit an Intent to File for disability compensation." The SF-180 (Getting Your Ammo)

What is it? The Standard Form 180 is the request for your official military personnel records (OMPF). This contains your service history, awards, and deployment records that "prove" you were where you say you were. Essentially, it's like performing a Freedom of Information Act request, but for your personal military records.They will send you a CD/DVD disc with a digital copy of your records in about 6 months.

How to fill it out correctly:

Section I: Fill out your personal info (Name, SSN, Service Number, Branch).

Section II: Under "Information Requested," check the box for "All Service Medical Records" and "Other" (write in: Complete OMPF/Personnel File).

Section III (The Signature): You cannot use a digital signature. You must print the form, sign it with a real pen (wet signature), and then scan/mail/fax it.

Where to send it: Send it to the National Personnel Records Center (NPRC) in St. Louis. Fax is fastest

Why this matters: The VA often "misses" things. Having your own copy of your OMPF ensures that when we use the AI engine to map your disabilities, we have the hard proof of your deployments and incidents.
